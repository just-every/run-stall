# Bring existing product reviews

In the shop desk, open **Move in**, then **Bring your reviews**. Choose the export source and upload its CSV. Run a dry run, inspect the dates and historical verification claims, then match any unmatched product. Each orphan names its reason. Re-map it to an existing product or explicitly exclude its row. Applying requires a fresh preview and explicit review of the rows and exclusions.

Judge.me and Yotpo exports retain their documented column names. Okendo and Loox templates are also accepted. Stamped accepts mapped common CSV fields and its named review fields; legacy Shopify Product Reviews accepts the common CSV fields. The precise accepted columns are documented in `src/stall/reviews/parse.ts`. Unsupported columns confer no authority or consent. Use at most 100 reviews and 20 media files per reviewed batch, with at most 2 MiB of UTF-8 CSV and 700,000 bytes per media file. Split larger exports before importing.

Products match by handle, SKU, native ID or an explicitly reviewed source product ID map. An ambiguous match needs a human choice. Explicit source product mappings are retained for subsequent exports and move with the installation archive. The later full-catalog migration clauses inherit supplying their source product maps to this door. Duplicate identity is the source app and source review ID; when no ID exists, it is a hash of the original review content, author and product reference. Re-running skips existing identities, without replacing later facts or fetching their media again.

The human owner or administrator applies and undoes imports. The preview never exposes the reviewer's email. The stored email is private business data, reserved for future review-request eligibility under CUSTOMER-002; importing never grants marketing consent, creates an account, emits a conversion or sends mail. REVIEWS-001 inherits native collection, moderation and consented requests. The small merchant-entry HTTP door records an explicitly unverified native review; it does not establish a purchase.

Before any review is applied, all its images and videos must copy successfully into Stall's database. Supported byte types are PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, MP4 and WebM. Import uses the existing public HTTPS endpoint validation, without credentials or redirects. An inaccessible or unsupported file refuses the whole batch; keep the source app until the import succeeds. INTEGRATION-001 retains the shared connection-level DNS binding obligation. The resulting public media path serves only while its review's product is published. Undo and erasure remove the corresponding owned files.

Product pages show the original review date, display name, text, reply, rating and source label. A verified claim is labeled with the source app, and every imported review has `verifiedPurchase: false`. Yotpo's Verified Reviewer means email verification, not a verified purchase; only Verified Buyer is retained as that app's purchase claim. An absent Judge.me verification field remains unverified. Product, Review and AggregateRating JSON-LD comes from current published native records in the initial HTML. An unpublished product exposes neither reviews nor review media.

Review import history allows a confirmed undo of a specific batch. Undo deletes only that batch's imported reviews and files; native reviews and other imports remain. The complete installation archive carries review records, original source identities, product mappings and owned bytes. The owner-only business JSON export includes review facts and private email. The existing customer erasure command removes that contact's reviews and files, invalidates old transfers, and keeps an opaque suppression digest so an old review export cannot reintroduce the erased contact.

The committed Judge.me and Yotpo fixtures are synthetic exports in the documented formats. Network-edge assertions in `tests/api/imports.test.mjs` prove source retirement and owned bytes. The emitted Worker journey `tests/ui/review-import-journey.mjs` proves the review desk and product page at 1440 and 390; it omits the source photo from its browser upload because the API assertions cover copying without live provider dependence. No provider API authorization or live-provider receipt is claimed by these export journeys; authorized API adapters remain an integration obligation of MIGRATE-001 and REVIEWS-001.
